### REQUEST NO. 8
*All communications between YOU and any person RELATING TO YOUR finances, this dissolution proceeding, or the division of property.*

**RESPONSE TO REQUEST NO. 8:**

**Objection 1 — attorney-client privilege and work product.** The Request, read with Definition 1 (which defines "YOU" to include Responding Party's attorneys and accountants), seeks communications between Responding Party and his counsel of record and counsel's staff. It also seeks communications among counsel and a consultant retained by counsel to assist in this proceeding.

- These communications are protected by the attorney-client privilege (Evid. Code, §§ 952, 954).
- Writings reflecting counsel's impressions, conclusions, opinions, or research, and other writings prepared by or for counsel in this proceeding, are protected by the attorney work-product doctrine (Code Civ. Proc., § 2018.030).
- The documents not produced on these grounds are identified in Attachment B (Entries 1–9).

**Objection 2 — vague, overbroad, and unduly burdensome.**

- **"YOUR finances."** Read literally, this phrase reaches every communication over more than ten years concerning any payment, purchase, or account. That includes routine merchant confirmations, marketing messages, and everyday household exchanges. The phrase is not reasonably particularized (Code Civ. Proc., § 2031.030, subd. (c)(1)).
- **"Any person."** This phrase is unlimited.

**Objection 3 — third-party privacy.** To the extent the Request seeks communications with third parties about matters unrelated to the parties' assets, debts, income, or the issues in this proceeding, it invades the constitutional privacy interests of those third parties (Cal. Const., art. I, § 1).

**Extent of compliance.** Subject to these objections, Responding Party will comply **in part**. He construes this Request to seek non-privileged communications from January 1, 2016, through the date of this response between Responding Party and any of the following persons:

- (a) Petitioner;
- (b) any bank, lender, credit card issuer, escrow or title company, employer, or other financial institution;
- (c) any tenant, vehicle purchaser, or vehicle seller; or
- (d) any family member or other third party.

The communications must concern the parties' assets, debts, income, or expenses, any payments toward or gifts of property, this proceeding, or the division of property. All such communications in his possession, custody, or control will be produced, including:

- Bank A letter dated October 30, 2026: DOC-002.
- Bank B secure message dated October 31, 2026: DOC-004.
- [Additional communications located through the search described in Note N16, to be identified by production number.] [CONFIRM–N16]

## C. Privilege and Work Product — Analysis for Counsel

### N22 — Privilege log format (decision required)

- **The RFP's demand:** Instruction 2 demands a log with date, author, recipients, subject matter, and privilege.
- **The governing rule:** CCP § 2031.240(c)(1) requires "sufficient factual information" to evaluate the claim, "including, if necessary, a privilege log." A log is not automatically required in every circumstance, nor never required (L6.5).
- **Draft approach:** Attachment B is an item-level log with neutral descriptions. Its subject-matter descriptions are generic so that it reveals no advice, admissions, or analysis. In particular, it does not reveal:
  - the move-money question;
  - the car sale prices or the cash;
  - the safe deposit contents;
  - counsel's reimbursement concession;
  - the gift theory; or
  - the accountant's figures or dissipation view.
- **Alternative:** A categorical description for post-service communications with litigation counsel and counsel's consultant, for example: "Communications between Respondent and his counsel of record and counsel's staff and retained consultant, October 29, 2026 to present, concerning this proceeding." Many practitioners use this approach. It risks a challenge that it does not provide "sufficient factual information."
- **Decision needed:** choose the format. Even if no log is served, an internal record should be kept.

### N23 — Merits of each protection claim (L6.1–L6.6)

The "PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL" labels on DOC-016 and DOC-018 are not dispositive. The analysis turns on purpose, participants, and confidentiality.

**DOC-009, DOC-011, DOC-022 (client to paralegal)**
- These are confidential communications from the client to the attorney's agent, made to obtain legal services (Evid. Code §§ 952, 954).
- They are likely privileged.
- The **underlying facts** they contain are not privileged: no receipts, no car paperwork, the lease missing, and Renter A's identity. Those facts are disclosed through the inability statements.

**DOC-015 and DOC-020 (client to attorney)**
- These are requests for advice and are privileged.
- The facts they reveal (car prices, $20,000 cash, box contents) must still be disclosed through the proper channels (N14, N15, N26).

**DOC-016 (attorney to client)**
- This is legal advice, and is privileged.
- It also reflects counsel's assessment of the House 1 reimbursement exposure and strategy, which is core work product (CCP § 2018.030(a)).

**DOC-017 (call scheduling)**
- This is a confidential lawyer-client communication. Under § 952 it is likely privileged regardless of how mundane its content.
- It is arguably not even "relating to" finances or division. It is logged out of caution.
- **Decision:** log it, or treat it as non-responsive.

**DOC-018 and DOC-019 (Accountant Y)**
- DOC-016 indicates that counsel engaged Accountant Y ("I'm also asking Accountant Y…"), and DOC-018 recites that the work was "prepared at the request of counsel."
- **Confirm that the engagement runs through counsel** (an engagement letter from the firm). If it does:
  - The analyses are work product, and the impressions and opinions in them have absolute protection: the preliminary principal split, the gift caution, and the "ordinary savings, not dissipation" view (CCP § 2018.030(a); *National Steel Products Co. v. Superior Court* (1985) 164 Cal.App.3d 476 — verify before citing).
  - They are also likely privileged under § 952, because disclosure to the consultant was reasonably necessary to accomplish counsel's purpose.
- **Copying the client:** Copying John on DOC-018 does not waive protection; he is the holder.
- **Expert designation risk:** If Accountant Y is later designated as an expert witness (CCP § 2034.210 et seq.), protection over the expert's work and reports may be lost or narrowed. Keep this in mind before designation, and consider whether a separate, non-testifying consultant is preferable.

Ensure everything given to the accountant is also produced if responsive. Underlying business records do not become protected by being sent to a consultant (L6.1).
