# Legal Research Provenance: From Sources to the Teaching Outline

Publication documentation added 2026-10-24 · Does not change frozen model inputs, legal principles, grading references or scores

## What “supplied principles” means in this study

The outline was the product of author-led legal research and synthesis, with legal-AI and general-AI assistance. It was not an arbitrary list generated solely for the test. The author formulated a hypothetical California discovery research problem, consulted LexisNexis Lexis+ Protégé and Westlaw CoCounsel, reviewed statutes and case opinions, compared propositions with primary text, and organized the resulting distinctions into a teaching outline. Law-firm discovery experience informed questions and stated drafting practices, but is not a substitute for authority or comprehensive attorney review.

The research questions asked for rules, elements, burdens, exceptions, facts that do not decide the outcome, facts that could change the outcome, leading authorities with pinpoint citations, statewide/local distinctions and unsettled points. This structure helped translate legal research into conditions a model could apply to new fictional facts. The supplied outline is available as [the actual model learning material](new%20docs/principles.md); the [Base task](new%20docs/opus5.5/task-prompt.md) and [Base actual message record](new%20docs/opus5.5/sets/base/model-messages.json) establish what the drafter received.

## Research and interpretation workflow

1. **Frame the research problem.** Identify California RFP-response issues rather than ask for a generic legal answer: scope, response dispositions, diligent search, document holders, objections, privilege/work product and human verification.
2. **Use research platforms for discovery, not final authority.** Lexis+ Protégé and Westlaw CoCounsel surfaced proposed rules and case citations. The author’s private exports document the questions and outputs; a vendor-generated summary or citator label was not treated as an opinion or a live certified status check.
3. **Read and compare primary texts.** The author’s research audit records statute/opinion checks and corrections. Examples include distinguishing a timely privilege objection from the adequacy of a log, avoiding an irrelevant case for a production-format proposition, and limiting Ryan to its historical interrogatory context. The source record also preserves uncertainty and instances where a previous author specification needed correction.
4. **Interpret with context and exceptions.** Separate a holding from analogy, the text of the assigned request from a general rule, discoverability from separate disclosure duties, and applicable legal requirements from author practice preferences. A cited authority is not proof that every later paraphrase or application is correct.
5. **Synthesize the outline.** Organize the legal material into scope, response/production, inability/search, objections, protection and consequence topics, alongside expressly identified professional-judgment safeguards: evidence is not an instruction, consequential decisions require human authority, and external text must be separated from counsel-only review.
6. **Keep teaching and assessment separate.** The drafter received the outline and learning example; the rubric, case-specific key and later adjudications were judge/author materials. Post-run reference disagreements remain disclosed rather than rewritten into what the drafter supposedly learned.

## Source provenance and review limits

The author’s private research collection includes commercial legal-research exports, case texts and statutory PDFs. Two checked export titles are “AI Assistant Response Disvoery Principles” (Lexis+ Protégé) and “WestLaw and cocounsel Research.” Their contents were inspected locally for this provenance clarification; the licensed files and any personal metadata are not distributed in this release. No client files are included. The research audit also records checks against public statutory and opinion text and corrections to both AI-proposed propositions and earlier author specifications.

Research-platform use and documented primary-text checking are distinct from independent attorney validation. The author is not a licensed attorney, and no comprehensive attorney review of the outline or adjudications is claimed. The report’s disclaimer still applies. Remaining source/application disagreements identified during Base review stay visible, including House 3 uncertainty and the task fit of R7/R10 questions.

The professional-safety layer also drew on AI governance/reliability literature and stated design choices. That lineage must not be confused with a completed EU AI Act compliance analysis or with a test of every principle in the outline. A complete empirical capability claim requires behavior on the relevant task, not merely a citation in the teaching material.
